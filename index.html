Tap any word or phrase to see a picture, the transcription /…/, British audio 🔊, a simple definition and the translation.

🧠 Grammar reference: verb be — I and you

Full formContraction (short form)
➕I am Helen. / You are Tom.I’m Helen. / You’re Tom.
➖I am not Ellen. / You are not Dom.I’m not Ellen. / You aren’t Dom. (or You’re not)
❓Am I in class 2? / Are you Mike?Yes, I am. / No, I’m not. / Yes, you are. / No, you aren’t.
Word order in questions: the verb be goes before the person.
I’m in class 2. → Am I in class 2?  •  You’re Tom. → Are you Tom?
Say: I’m Helen.   NOT I Helen.   NOT Am Helen.

Examples from our lesson: “Hello. Are you Helen?” • “Yes, I am. And you’re Tom.” • “No, I’m not. I’m Helen.”

≈ 8 min Speaking

Role-play: at the school café ☕

Practise the conversation with your teacher. Then swap roles. Tick ✅ the steps when you do them!

🧑 Role A · Barista

  • Greet: Hello! / Hi!
  • Ask: What’s your name?
  • Check: Are you …?
  • Give the drink: Your tea / cappuccino.
  • Say goodbye: Goodbye! See you on …

🎒 Role B · New student

  • Greet: Hi! I’m …
  • Answer: Yes, I am. / No, I’m not. I’m …
  • Order: A tea / A cappuccino / Two teas, please. (use a number! 🔢)
  • Say: Nice to meet you.
  • Say goodbye: Bye! See you tomorrow.
